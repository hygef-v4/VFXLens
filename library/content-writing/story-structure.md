# Story Structure for Explainers

## Choose the Structure by the Question

| Structure | Useful question | Reference evidence |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Follow an object through a system | How does something move, change ownership or acquire a new state? | The [Nvidia case](../case-studies/nvidia-chips-cartoon-explainer.md) follows a box/receipt through its studied opening and later returns to shipping labels/maps. |
| Sort cases by evidence, then synthesize | Are similar-looking events supported by the same explanation? | The [Internet case](../case-studies/internet-global-explainer.md) repeatedly returns to a three-column classification board, with separate status/claim cards. |
| Explain a mechanism, then expose its scale limit | Does a local explanation account for the larger outcome? | The Nvidia ASR around 171–318s moves from small transport examples to their claimed limits before introducing the next mechanism. |

These are alternative structures. Choose or combine them when the subject calls for it; do not force every video through the same sequence.

## Build an Explanatory Chain

1. Start from the question and payoff recorded in [Hooks and Promises](hooks-and-promises.md).
2. List the steps a viewer needs to understand the answer. For each step, identify the question it resolves, evidence it uses and question that follows.
3. Introduce entities and terms before relying on them. Keep one object, map, relationship or recap layout available as a continuity anchor.
4. Use comparisons to make scale intelligible. Check that units, dates, denominators and the comparison itself remain valid after simplification.
5. Place a recap where several cases or relationships need to be carried forward. It should expose a useful conclusion or distinction, not merely repeat a list.
6. Close by answering the original question. Add actions or a new open question when appropriate; state uncertainty when the evidence does not supply a definitive answer.

Use causal language only when supported. A sequence of events or matching dates does not itself demonstrate cause. Keep claims and attribution visible in the script as well as the image; the [evidence guide](../foundations/evidence-and-validation.md) defines the project's availability states.

## Questions, Dialogue and Transitions

Short questions can introduce a genuinely missing step, and a second character can voice the audience's likely objection. Give the next beat an answer or an explicit reason it is not yet known. A rhetorical question without a useful answer is not automatically a narrative improvement.

Link sections with a consequence, limitation, comparison or unresolved question. The Nvidia study illustrates a change of mechanism after a scale objection; the Internet study illustrates revisiting a classification after more cases. Keep source-specific locators and wording in their case studies.

If a promotional segment is present, mark it separately in the outline. Identify where the story is suspended and how it returns to the same question/object; product claims remain promotional testimony until verified. The Nvidia study records such a segment within its first ten minutes.

## Completion Check

The outline is ready to draft when each necessary entity is introduced, each section has a reason to follow the previous one, the promise has a payoff, and unsupported claims/remaining unknowns are marked. A structure inferred from an ASR transcript is a reference-study finding, not proof of the original author's intent or audience response.
