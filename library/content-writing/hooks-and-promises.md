# Hooks and Promises

## Purpose

A hook establishes what is unusual, why it matters and what question the video will answer. Its promise should be specific enough to guide the rest of the script. Curiosity is a proposed writing goal; neither transcript analysis nor an attractive opening proves audience retention.

## Patterns Supported by the Current References

| Pattern | Reference observation | How to adapt |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Familiar situation, larger question | The [Internet explainer](../case-studies/internet-global-explainer.md) opens on work devices and later groups several incident illustrations into a recap/category board. | Begin with an experience the audience can recognize; explain which larger question the examples actually support. |
| Concrete metaphor and unexpected scale | The [Nvidia explainer](../case-studies/nvidia-chips-cartoon-explainer.md) pairs a love-letter image with the opening transaction story in its ASR. | Choose one understandable metaphor, then reveal the property that makes the subject worth explaining. Keep any factual magnitude sourced. |
| Question, correction, new route | The Nvidia ASR opening asks a possible explanation, rejects it, then proposes following a box/receipt. | Correct a plausible misunderstanding using evidence, then give the viewer a concrete path through the explanation. |

These are observations from selected reference intervals, not complete or listening-verified scripts. Read each case's coverage and attribution limits before quoting it.

## Drafting Method

1. Write the core answer first. Identify the audience's likely starting knowledge and the missing piece the video can explain.
2. Choose one opening object, situation or contrast that leads directly to that answer. Avoid introducing several mysteries with no shared payoff.
3. State the central question and the route to answering it. Draft a first visual that can communicate the same subject as the narration.
4. Check the proposed opening against the evidence. Stronger language must not turn a source allegation into a fact or an unknown into a dramatic certainty.
5. Trace the promise to its eventual answer in the outline. Revise either end when they do not match.

## New Creative Example

For a fictional explainer about a delayed parcel: **a receipt says “delivered,” but the box is still on a conveyor**. The promise is to follow the status changes that created the mismatch. The first shot, question and planned explanation refer to the same object. This is a new writing exercise, not a claim extracted from either reference.

## Review

Check the opening at the intended spoken pace and image size. Keep the key object readable and identify where the full promise becomes clear. Select timing for the new narration; there is no measured universal hook duration in these studies. Remove a provocative claim when the later evidence cannot support it.
