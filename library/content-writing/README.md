# Content Writing for Video

Reusable knowledge for writing the ideas, narration and structure behind a video. This section owns the script; [styles](../styles/README.md) own the visual language and [techniques](../techniques/README.md) own execution. An effective script should still communicate its reasoning before effects are added.

## Use in Production

1. State the audience, one main question and the answer the video will deliver.
2. Assemble a sourced outline. Mark facts, source allegations, interpretations, unknowns and new creative choices distinctly.
3. Choose a hook and structure suited to that question. Draft a narration beat for each necessary step in the explanation.
4. Give each beat a visual job; then test whether the script remains clear when spoken aloud and whether its promise is paid off.
5. Move the resulting script and visual beats into the existing production checkpoint/storyboard through the [production workflow](../../workflows/create-video.md). For motion-graphics explainers, use its [specialized route](../../workflows/ai-assisted-motion-graphics.md).

Keep drafts, raw transcripts, claim/source ledgers and episode-specific documents in the private production workspace. Maintain the current script there rather than creating a second authoritative script in the library. Public articles contain reusable methods and links to evidence, not copied narration or personal source paths.

## Learning from References

Use the [study workflow](../../workflows/study-reference.md): approximately ten minutes is the initial learning scope for a long video, with a timestamped transcript and selected image/motion examples. A transcript helps identify structure; ASR wording/times are inferred until checked. A visible technique or rhetorical pattern does not establish that it improved retention or caused the video's success.

The current examples are the [character-led Nvidia explainer](../case-studies/nvidia-chips-cartoon-explainer.md) and [evidence-led Internet explainer](../case-studies/internet-global-explainer.md). These illustrate different approaches, not a universal formula.

## Review Before Production

- Does the hook describe the actual subject, and does the ending answer its question?
- Does each section add an explanation, evidence, a useful comparison or a payoff?
- Are allegations and unknowns still distinct from established facts after simplification? See [Evidence and Validation](../foundations/evidence-and-validation.md).
- Can a viewer follow the people, objects, quantities and causal steps without relying on decorative motion?
- Are narration timing, original quotes and factual claims checked to the level reported? Describe missing checks explicitly.
