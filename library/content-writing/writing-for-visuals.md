# Writing for Visuals

## Purpose

Turn a spoken explanation into scenes that show a relationship, action, change or piece of evidence. The script owns the meaning; the storyboard adds composition and frame timing through the [production workflow](../../workflows/ai-assisted-motion-graphics.md). Keep the current script in that production's existing checkpoint or linked draft.

## Give Each Beat a Visual Job

| The script needs to explain… | Possible visual job |
| --- | --- |
| A person, object or unfamiliar term | Introduce a character/prop with a short identifying label |
| A change of state or action | Show the same object before, during and after the change |
| Movement through locations or owners | Follow a marked object along a route or handoff |
| Chronology or conflicting updates | Place sourced statements on one timeline |
| Magnitude or a comparison | Show repeatable units, a scale or a labeled chart with a valid denominator |
| Attribution, evidence or an unknown | Separate source statements and status; show missing data as missing |
| A relationship or mechanism | Build a diagram or spatial metaphor in explanatory order |
| A practical next step | Show the action on a recognizable object/UI, with its limitation if needed |

The [Nvidia case](../case-studies/nvidia-chips-cartoon-explainer.md) illustrates objects, handoffs and questions; the [Internet case](../case-studies/internet-global-explainer.md) illustrates classification, timelines and explicit uncertainty. These support visual options, not a requirement to imitate their characters or assets.

## Drafting and Revision

1. Write one clear idea per beat and identify its subject. Replace vague pronouns when several people or objects could be meant.
2. Choose what the viewer should see change or learn in that beat. Split sentences that require unrelated images at the same time.
3. Assign work between picture and speech. Use speech for reasoning and context; let the image make the relevant relationship visible. Preserve essential labels, sources and accessibility needs rather than removing all repetition mechanically.
4. Mark authentic evidence, reconstructed UI and illustrative metaphors distinctly. A generated document must not masquerade as an original source screenshot.
5. Read the draft aloud against a rough visual sequence. Move a reveal to its actual narration cue; keep estimated durations marked until audio is available. Check the readable hold after text completion, not just its entrance.
6. Transfer the beats to the shared storyboard with cue IDs. Use the chosen [isometric](../styles/isometric-explainer.md) or [cartoon](../styles/cartoon-explainer.md) guide for image/motion decisions instead of defining a second visual specification in the script.

## New Creative Example

For the fictional parcel scenario from [Hooks and Promises](hooks-and-promises.md), one beat establishes the box and its status label, the next traces the scan that changed the status, and the last compares the label with the physical location. The narration explains what each status means; the picture keeps the same marked box visible. These are proposed shots for a writing exercise, not observed source scenes.

## Review and Limits

Check terminology, spoken rhythm, drawable actions, attribution, cue order and text density before detailed animation. Inspect at the target viewing size and with actual audio. ASR segment boundaries are navigation aids, not exact production cue points. No universal word-count-per-shot, Vietnamese speaking rate or reading-time formula has been established by the current case studies.
